Model definition

w_j(t_k) = reported_size_j × 2^(-(t_k - t_j) / 30 minutes)
log P0(t_k) = sum_j w_j(t_k) × adjusted_log_price_j / sum_j w_j(t_k)
r_observed_bp(t) = 10,000 × (P_observed(t) / P0(t−) − 1)
r_predicted_bp(t) = fitted regression of r_observed_bp on available features
P1(t) = P0(t−) × (1 + r_predicted_bp(t) / 10,000)
  • Eligible bond executions and target clocks use 10:00 ≤ time < 15:30 US Eastern. Prior-day states can carry forward across sessions.
  • P0 smooths log prices with a 30-minute calendar-time half-life and reported historical trade-size weights. Same-timestamp trades are handled together; the target trade never enters its own anchor.
  • The transaction-side adjustment is calibrated separately for each forecast month from observations available before that month. January starts with a zero-adjustment fallback. Earlier training anchors retain the calibration that was available when they were formed; they are not refiltered using later information.
  • Trade-size caps supply disclosed lower bounds, not exact large-trade quantities. Volume weighting is therefore a recorded-size proxy, and is not a guarantee that larger trades are more informative.
  • The time-only P0 comparator gives each simultaneous batch unit weight and uses its unweighted adjusted log-price mean. The last-observation comparator is the raw size-weighted geometric price of the latest available batch; it does not impose an arbitrary ordering on simultaneous executions.
  • The regression predicts a simple clean-price return from P0, in basis points. The prediction is converted back to price using that single anchor.
  • Sector and broad rating define categories and market-sensitivity interactions in a pooled regression. Liquidity is ranked within the joint category using only the preceding completed month's activity. Ranking does not itself remove less-liquid bonds.
  • The numerical controls include available ETF movement, staleness, clock time, lagged size and liquidity information, duration and the lagged 10-year Treasury yield level/change, including duration × rate-change exposure. See the feature dictionary for the exact fitted field list and units.
  • A duration must come from the latest earlier dated calculation, pass its numerical/price screen and be no more than seven calendar days old. Missing or failed latest values are not replaced by older passing ones. Missing duration is imputed from training observations with a missingness indicator, so missing duration alone does not remove a target from the sample.
  • The ridge penalty is fixed at alpha=100; model preprocessing and regression coefficients use only earlier training observations.

Interpretation and limitations

  • Forecast availability and execution time are distinct. Historical bond observations must be received before the forecast. Staleness and ETF movement use the price's execution-time anchor, not the later assumed receipt time. A fixed delay is a scenario assumption, not proof of the actual dissemination timestamp.
  • Daily Treasury and duration observations must predate the forecast date. The same day's closing rate or yield is unavailable for an earlier intraday forecast. Generic Treasury yields are rate benchmarks rather than a complete discount curve.
  • Duration is a contractual fixed-cash-flow maturity proxy. Most bonds are callable; this is not effective or option-adjusted duration. Current Bloomberg bond terms, sector classifications and rating labels make this a retrospective experiment, even with chronological target alignment.
  • Reported transaction prices include execution noise. Lower errors against TRACE prints do not establish observation of latent fair value. The analysis is price estimation, not a trading backtest or a net-return strategy.
  • The historical dataset and evaluation period have already been inspected. Hyperparameters should be frozen before a newly acquired period is used as a prospective test.

Discussion and next experiments

  • Compare the time-only and time-and-size P0 baselines first: their difference isolates whether recorded-size weighting helps on this sample. Compare P1 against its time-and-size P0 anchor to assess the incremental value of the return regression.
  • Use monthly and liquidity-group results to distinguish a broad improvement from a result driven by a few active bonds or volatile dates. The large-error illustration is deliberately retained alongside a typical case so model failures remain visible.
  • Useful next tests are verified historical ratings and contract terms, actual TRACE dissemination timestamps, alternative size-weight transforms, and rate sensitivities that account for optionality. Change one specification at a time and assess it on matched later observations.
